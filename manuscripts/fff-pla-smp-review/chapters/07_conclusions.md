# 7. Conclusions

Fused-filament polylactic acid stores a temporary shape when its amorphous phase is programmed and releases that shape when the phase is mobilized again. In the open 2022–2026 studies, the temperatures that do this work lie between about 40 °C for a polyethylene-glycol-plasticized cellulose-nanocrystal composite and about 80–85 °C for hot recovery or hot training of unfilled hinges (Hacıoğlu et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2025; Umayanga et al., 2026). Programming temperature moves fixity by several percentage points across a few degrees and moves cycle life in the opposite direction from actuation speed (Hacıoğlu et al., 2026; Huang et al., 2025; Honari et al., 2025). Blend ratio moves elongation from the teens to more than 200% and changes recovery on a scale that printing speed, in the study that ranked it, does not match (Hamidi et al., 2026; Rahmatabadi et al., 2024). Layer thickness is the deposition setting most clearly tied to recovery ratio, and even there the confirmed gain is small (Hamidi et al., 2026). Raster angle and build orientation control mechanical anisotropy and, when the raster is a continuous flax yarn or a magnetized bead, control the direction of actuation (Baharlou & Ma, 2025; de Kergariou et al., 2023; Moradi et al., 2023; Toprak et al., 2026).

Fiber reinforcement belongs in this picture as a structural net-point only where the interface holds. Continuous silk, well-impregnated flax, and coated kenaf raise strength or modulus by large factors, and silk and flax architectures have now been tied to compressive recovery or to hygroscopic curvature (Beg et al., 2024; de Kergariou et al., 2023; Hamat et al., 2025; Rahmani et al., 2026). Short carbon fiber more often arrives in the open mechanical literature as a source of porosity and weak interlayer planes, with modulus gains in some recycled-fiber filaments and with strength or fatigue losses in several printed coupons (Bermudo Gamboa et al., 2023; Gajjar et al., 2026; Saleh et al., 2024; Vălean et al., 2024b). Continuous-carbon impregnation is being quantified (Huang et al., 2026; Sankar et al., 2025). Its shape-fixity and multi-cycle recovery are not yet quantified in this open set. Hydrothermal aging can destroy a large fraction of flax-composite strength while leaving most of the shape-memory ratios intact (Bouguermouh et al., 2026). Memory and load-bearing are separable, which is why both have to be reported.

Relative to the broad account of polylactic acid blends, mechanisms, and applications given by Aghabarari et al. (2025), the distinctive result of the present review is a process ranking grounded in open experiments. Temperature of programming and recovery, matrix ductility, and fiber continuity dominate. Nozzle speed and infill percentage do not yet have a comparable actuation literature, even though both are mature variables for strength. Devices that the open papers actually close—the hinge, the gripper, the bio-screw, the foam, the cellular spring, and the flax hygromorph—show that fiber-reinforced and blended polylactic acid is already a practical 4D-printing feedstock (de Kergariou et al., 2023; Desole et al., 2026; Doostmohammadi et al., 2024; Honari et al., 2025; Huang et al., 2025; Umayanga et al., 2026). It becomes a reliable one when recovery is still measured at the fiftieth cycle, on a specimen whose fiber angle and interface quality were measured as carefully as its first return.
