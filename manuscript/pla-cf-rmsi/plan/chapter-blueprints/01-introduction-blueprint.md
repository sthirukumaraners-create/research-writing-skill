# Introduction blueprint

Target file: chapters/01-introduction.md
Minimum: 4500 non-whitespace characters and 6 prose paragraphs.
No bullet lists. No process notes. Original sentences.

### Paragraph 1
- Role: context
- Main claim: FFF is used for functional polymer parts, but interlayer bonding still limits mechanical reliability, and fiber-filled filaments make property prediction less transferable.
- Evidence IDs: S1, S2
- Contrast or transition: move from the general process limit to the parameters that change measured strength.
- Forbidden content: do not claim this study measured bond width, porosity, or temperature history.

### Paragraph 2
- Role: method landscape
- Main claim: Layer thickness and raster angle have changed tensile strength of FFF PLA, and layer height has also changed flexural and impact strength of short-carbon-fiber PLA.
- Evidence IDs: S3, S4
- Contrast or transition: those gains are property-specific, which sets up the fiber tradeoff.
- Forbidden content: do not transfer Gajjar's percentages onto the present PLA-CF data.

### Paragraph 3
- Role: limitation
- Main claim: Carbon-fiber addition does not raise every strength. Reported studies show tensile gains with flexural losses, flexural gains against neat PLA, brittle response, and losses when fibers agglomerate.
- Evidence IDs: S5, S7, S8, S9, S10
- Contrast or transition: because the present record has no neat-PLA control, these papers are context rather than a benchmark for the nine runs.
- Forbidden content: do not say the Bambu Lab filament is stronger than neat PLA.

### Paragraph 4
- Role: method landscape
- Main claim: Infill density changes chopped carbon-fiber PLA strength, and raster orientation changes both tension and flexure in PLA+, so this screen holds infill fixed and varies raster angle together with layer thickness and speed.
- Evidence IDs: S11, S6
- Contrast or transition: neither of those studies supplies the compression column used here.
- Forbidden content: do not equate Ramírez-Prieto's 0°/0° raster with the single 0° code in this L9.

### Paragraph 5
- Role: bottleneck
- Main claim: Studies that already combine responses, or that measure tension, compression, and flexure, still obtain winners that depend on the response and on the aggregation rule.
- Evidence IDs: S12, S13, S14, S15
- Contrast or transition: an explicit equal-weight index and a separate worst-case score make that dependence visible.
- Forbidden content: do not describe grey relational analysis, TOPSIS, or desirability as invalid.

### Paragraph 6
- Role: gap
- Main claim: The missing object is a declared min-max, equal-weight score for short-carbon-fiber PLA across tensile, compressive, and flexural strength, set beside a maximin score, on one unreplicated L9 screen.
- Evidence IDs: S13, S16, S17
- Contrast or transition: state what the index can and cannot decide.
- Forbidden content: do not call the index an optimum for service loads outside the nine runs.

### Paragraph 7
- Role: contribution
- Main claim: This article calculates that index for layer thickness, printing speed, and raster angle, reports both rankings, and keeps the conclusion inside the screening use.
- Evidence IDs: none required beyond the study definition
- Contrast or transition: end without a section-by-section roadmap.
- Forbidden content: no list of contributions.
